Army First Aid Symptom Sorter

Source: Army ATP 4-02.11, March 2026 (supersedes TC 4-02.1) · public release, distribution unlimited
Design prototype. Built from one Army manual, ATP 4-02.11, to test the interface. The ranking is a simple, unvalidated match score. Do not use it to make decisions about a real casualty.

    How the ranking works, and what this prototype leaves out

    Score. Each sign gets a weight: signs listed under many conditions (dizziness, nausea) count less than signs listed under few (hot dry skin, rigid abdomen). For each condition the page computes two things:

    • Explains: the weighted share of your entries that the condition lists.
    • Matches: the weighted share of the condition's listed signs that you entered.

    The match score combines the two but counts Explains twice as heavily as Matches. A cause that accounts for everything you saw should outrank one that fits only part of it, because casualties rarely show every sign the manual lists. Situation chips you haven't entered don't count against a condition. The score is not a probability. When scores tie, conditions the manual says to evacuate sooner rank higher.

    Situation chips (dashed outline) are not signs listed in the manual. They come from each chapter's scope, such as "hot environment" for the heat illness chapter. They carry the same weight as signs.

    Evacuation tags summarize the manual's wording: "Evacuate immediately" (MEDEVAC or immediate evacuation), "Medical help immediately", "Evacuate" (as soon as possible), "Seek medical aid", or "No evacuation required" (seek care only if symptoms worsen). The full wording is in each treatment panel.

    Not included

    • Military and procedural chapters (Ch 1–3 casualty response and TCCC phases, Ch 15 monitoring and evacuation, Ch 19 CBRN, Ch 20 combat stress) and Ch 18 (sickle cell trait) are left out.
    • Scorpions get no signs or treatment in this manual, so they are left out. Centipede and millipede stings, which the manual calls minor, are left out too.
    • Tier 2 skills meant for trained combat lifesavers (needle decompression, detailed wound packing) are left out; pelvic fracture is included because falls can cause it.
    • Asthma: the manual mentions it only as a risk factor and gives no first aid for it.
    • Signs quote the manual, lightly trimmed; treatment steps are condensed from it, and bleeding steps draw on several chapters.
