About this app

Source. Emergency Medical Services Protocols and Procedures, Field Manual #51, National Park Service and U.S. Forest Service, 2025 edition. FM-51's 55 protocols are split here into 58 conditions (for example AMS, HAPE and HACE within Altitude Illness), each with its own signs quoted from the manual.

Sign groups. The groups follow the assessment order in FM-51's ABCs: Universal Patient Care protocol (2001, p.108). The Primary and Secondary headings follow common wilderness first aid teaching, which puts airway, breathing, circulation and a quick mental status check (AVPU) in the primary assessment. FM-51's flowchart is stricter: its primary assessment covers life-threatening conditions and airway management, and it lists breathing rate, pulse, skin and mental status (AVPU/GCS) as vital signs in the secondary assessment. Group names and chip wording are the app's own; FM-51's exact wording shows when you open a condition.

How ranking works. Each sign gets a weight: signs listed under many conditions (nausea, dizziness) count less than signs listed under few (fruity breath, pinpoint pupils). For each condition the app computes:

  • Explains: the weighted share of your entries that the condition lists.
  • Matches: the weighted share of the condition's listed signs that you entered.

The score combines the two but counts Explains twice as heavily, because casualties rarely show every sign the manual lists. Situations you haven't entered don't count against a condition. Ties go to the more urgent condition. The main list shows at most five conditions scoring close to the top match; everything else that shares a sign is tucked under "Other conditions."

Urgency tags group conditions by FM-51's own wording (quoted under each condition): Urgent life threat or rapid transport, Evaluate needs medical evaluation or transport, Treat & watch field care and monitoring. The grouping is the app author's reading of that wording.

What the treatment tags mean

FM-51 marks each treatment step with the provider levels allowed to do it. The app shows only steps a patroller could do:

  • Any provider FM-51: "ALL Providers."
  • EMR level FM-51: Emergency Medical Responder, the lowest certified EMS level.
  • If trained FM-51 gives no level, or says "Perform as per your scope." Allowed if within your training (p.13); for IPWA patrollers, that's IPWA's training.
  • Needs doctor's OK FM-51: online medical control. A physician approves by radio or phone first.
  • Orange note FM-51's medication table (p.8) limits responders more than the flowchart, e.g. "only help the patient take their own."

Offline. After the first visit the app works without a signal.
